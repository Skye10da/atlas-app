505Mirren’s Thumb on the Spine

Mirren pressed her thumb to the ledger’s spine and felt the day exhale through it. Somewhere below the seventh floor, the small hours were being counted out like coins — one, two — and the arithmetic of it, she thought, was the truest weather Vessa had ever known.

She did not record the weather. She recorded what it did to the clerks: the way Pellamy smoked two cigarettes to end one, and the way Tam’s signature came off the page half-collapsed, pencil having run along the grain of his exhaustion.

“The margin,” she wrote, and then the word itself was out of ink, so she said it out loud, in the dark left ear of the vending machine. The ledgers slept without her. The government carried on breathing, quietly, under the door of the small hours where it wouldn’t be disturbed by what Mirren next weighed on the scale.
